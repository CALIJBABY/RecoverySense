# RecoverySense Evidence-to-Design Validation Matrix

## How to read this document

The cited literature supports design choices and reporting practices. It does **not** validate RecoverySense's predictive accuracy, clinical benefit, or safety. Those require protocol-approved human-subject data, independent evaluation, and physical-device testing.

| RecoverySense design choice | Evidence basis | What the evidence supports | What remains unvalidated in RecoverySense |
|---|---|---|---|
| Required repeated EMA is one 0–10 craving item on the watch | Ponnada et al. (2022) delivered single-question smartwatch micro-EMAs at high frequency; a prior comparison found microinteraction watch EMA outperformed longer watch EMA in response behavior. | A very short watch interaction is a defensible way to reduce burden and obtain temporally dense labels. | The exact wording, prompt rate, response distribution, acceptability, and validity for each behavioral-addiction population. |
| Preserve prompt, open, submit, source, and response-delay timestamps | Micro-EMA/EMA studies emphasize intensive longitudinal timing and response behavior. mERA calls for transparent reporting of intervention delivery and use. | Timing metadata are necessary to evaluate latency, adherence, and whether a label is close enough to its sensor window. | Acceptable delay thresholds and missing-response policy for the final protocol. |
| Combine passive physiology/activity, sleep context, and EMA | A 2026 gambling protocol uses 28 days of smartwatch physiology/activity/sleep plus brief craving/behavior EMAs; wearable-triggered JITAI research has also paired physiology with craving EMA. | The multimodal architecture is aligned with current addiction-research directions. | No universal physiological craving signature or validated RecoverySense trigger threshold exists. The gambling paper is a protocol, not evidence of achieved prediction performance. |
| Begin with processed BPM plus motion and add raw PPG later | Wearable research commonly combines cardiac and movement streams, but raw PPG access/quality differs by device. | A staged sensor-ablation plan is appropriate. | Whether PPG improves craving prediction beyond BPM, motion, sleep, and timing. |
| Call PPG interval features PRV, not automatically HRV | Kantrowitz et al. (2025) found PPG-derived PRV is not equivalent to ECG-derived HRV in a large clinical population. | Terminology must distinguish optical pulse intervals from ECG RR/NN intervals. | Agreement on the selected watch, placement, motion conditions, and behavioral-addiction population. |
| Use 30-second sleep epochs and treat sleep as a separate model | Fonseca et al. (2023) classified 30-second sleep epochs using PPG-derived cardiac activity and accelerometry against PSG; other wearable studies also use 30-second epochs and independent reference labels. | Thirty-second epoching and separate sleep preprocessing are consistent with wearable sleep research. | RecoverySense sleep/wake accuracy; true stage accuracy; generalization beyond collected participants. |
| Start with binary estimated sleep/wake | Four-stage wearable systems generally use richer cardiac/PPG inputs and EEG/PSG labels. | Binary sleep/wake is the more defensible initial target with BPM and wrist motion. | EEG/PSG-validated staging and diagnostic use. |
| Use participant-grouped, session-grouped, and purged temporal evaluation | TRIPOD+AI emphasizes transparent, rigorous prediction-model development/evaluation; leakage from correlated observations can inflate performance. | Entire participants/sessions and future time periods should remain outside training when possible. | External validation on an independent site/sample and prospective performance. |
| Report discrimination, calibration, class-specific errors, prompt burden, and missingness | TRIPOD+AI, DECIDE-AI, and mERA emphasize transparent reporting beyond a single accuracy value, including implementation and human factors. | Balanced accuracy, sensitivity, specificity, false-positive rate, AUROC, average precision, Brier score, false prompts/day, response rate, latency, and missingness are appropriate. | Minimum clinically/research acceptable values must be prespecified with the study team. |
| Keep rule/model triggers disabled until validated | DECIDE-AI stresses early live evaluation, safety, human factors, and actual workflow performance. | A demonstration rule should not be treated as a validated intervention. | Prospective false-prompt burden, missed-craving rate, user safety, and intervention effectiveness. |
